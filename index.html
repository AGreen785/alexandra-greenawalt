Project 01

Collective Impact Backbone + Enterprise Data Infrastructure

Pathways Community Center · York County, SC 20+ Organizations $850K Budget 2021–2024
The Problem

20+ health and human service agencies were operating in complete silos — sharing clients but not data, duplicating services, and losing people between referrals. There was no coordinated entry, no shared measurement, and no mechanism to close the loop when a client's need was met. Partners were competing for the same scarce grant dollars while unknowingly providing the same services to the same people.

Methodology & Approach

Applied the Collective Impact framework (FSG model) to design and operate a first-of-its-kind Community Care Hub — co-located service delivery with contractual partner alignment and unified intake. Used asset mapping and needs assessment to eliminate service duplication across 20+ agencies. Implemented Unite Us as enterprise data infrastructure after iterating through paper and custom-built solutions. Built a Results-Based Accountability measurement framework and ran monthly continuous quality improvement cycles. Created SOPs, training curricula, and technical assistance protocols to drive 100% adoption and ensure sustainability beyond leadership transition.

Project 02

Financial Navigation Program + Hybrid Reimbursement Sustainability Model

Carolina Blood & Cancer Care Associates · SC 1,000+ Cancer Patients DBA Dissertation Research
The Problem

The last remaining independent community oncology practice in South Carolina was losing $300K annually providing non-clinical SDOH care navigation to 1,000+ cancer patients — services they knew drove outcomes but couldn't sustain financially. The practice faced a binary choice: eliminate wraparound care or sell to a healthcare conglomerate. Neither was acceptable. No existing reimbursement model adequately addressed the gap.

Methodology & Approach

Conducted qualitative key informant interviews with billing specialists, physician-owners, and care navigators; performed thematic analysis to surface operational and financial gaps. Applied financial modeling to compare fee-for-service, value-based care, and blended finance scenarios. Redesigned end-to-end care navigation workflow using Lean process mapping — optimizing G-code and Z-code billing capture and reducing per-episode cost. Structured a pharmaceutical partnership strategy through the NOLA 501(c)(3) to close the remaining funding gap, creating a replicable hybrid reimbursement model.

Project 03

Volunteer Coalition → Funded Regional System

Catawba Area Coalition for the Homeless · Multi-County, SC 100+ Cross-Sector Partners Board Secretary
The Problem

A volunteer-run homelessness coalition had existed for 20+ years as a monthly networking meeting — no governance structure, no funding, no actionable agenda, no strategic plan. Partners were spinning the same wheels in separate task forces, speaking different sector languages, and making no measurable systems-level progress. Meanwhile, community homelessness was accelerating faster than individual organizations could respond.

Methodology & Approach

Applied Collective Impact framework and national benchmarking (Houston, TX "The Way Home," Greenville, SC "United Housing Coalition," Charlotte, NC "Roof Above") to redesign coalition governance from networking group to action-producing system. Used systems mapping and asset-based community development to design four strategic sub-committees (Innovate / Collaborate / Educate / Advocate). Produced the region's first-ever homelessness white paper — the strategic deliverable that established credibility with funders and policymakers and unlocked the coalition's first Executive Director hire.

Project 04

Statewide Opioid Emergency Response System

Keystone Substance Abuse Services · Statewide SC $5.7M Behavioral Health Portfolio 100+ Clinicians
The Problem

South Carolina was experiencing a rapid spike in fentanyl and opioid overdoses — fatal and non-fatal — with no real-time data infrastructure to track where incidents were occurring, no tiered communication system to alert responders, and no legislative framework to enable proportionate law enforcement response. Resources were deployed reactively, often weeks after hotspots had shifted. People were dying in the gap.

Methodology & Approach

Applied Strategic Prevention Framework (SPF) — assess, plan, implement, evaluate — to map stakeholder assets, identify data gaps, and design a tiered systems response. Used ecological social modeling to align cross-sector partners (law enforcement, hospitals, behavioral health, public health) around a shared real-time data infrastructure (ODMAP). Engineered a 3-tier communication network to convert data into timed emergency response, with 48-hour harm reduction deployment capability. Parallel-tracked policy advocacy, co-authoring bipartisan legislation while building community behavior change through a scalable training program.
